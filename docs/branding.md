# Rally Row: brand direction

This guide records the approved brand decisions. Use the production artwork linked
below for logo and favicon files. Earlier concept images and generation prompts
are historical references; their wording and typography may predate these decisions.

## Brand idea

Rally Row brings the energy of watching together to the moment of choosing a winner.
It gives everyday fans a clear read on a matchup while leaving the call in their hands.

The name suggests a section of the stands: a familiar place among fellow fans.
The visual identity and language should remain useful across sports and seasons.

## Positioning

For everyday fans and pool players, Rally Row makes sports matchup probabilities
easy to understand, so choosing a winner becomes an engaging part of game day.

Functional benefit: see which team the model favors and how close the matchup is.
Emotional benefit: feel informed, involved, and ready to join the conversation.

## Messaging

Selected tagline: **A little insight. A lot to talk about.**

Connect useful matchup insight with the fun of talking sports with fellow fans.
Keep tagline language informed, welcoming, and social; avoid betting associations.

Approved homepage headline: **What's your call?**

Approved homepage supporting copy:

See how the teams stack up and who's more likely to win. Bring a little more
insight to the game-day conversation.

Approved primary button label: **Explore matchups**

Approved short description:

Rally Row helps everyday sports fans see how teams stack up. It gives them the numbers to make informed calls.

Approved methodology summary:

Rally Row builds team ratings from past seasons and updates them as the current season progresses. For each matchup, we compare those ratings to show each team’s chance of winning.

## Voice

Sound like a knowledgeable friend watching the game alongside you. Keep the energy
in headlines and the precision in the numbers. Let fans make their own call.

| Situation | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Matchup heading | Who takes it? |
| Close matchup | This one's close. |
| Model favorite | Seattle is favored. |
| Probability explanation | A 60% chance of winning still leaves room for an upset. |
| Team comparison | See how they stack up. |
| Updating | Updating the matchup picture. |
| Update failure | We couldn't update the results. Your last saved predictions are still here. |

Use ordinary language in the main experience. Explain model details where fans
can choose to read them. In introductory copy, prefer "chance of winning" to
"win probability." Avoid taunts, dominance claims, betting slang, guarantees,
and unsupported claims of superior prediction accuracy.

## Approved visual direction

| Color | Hex | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Midnight navy | #101B2D | Primary logo background, brand header, favicon tile; text on light surfaces |
| Electric blue | #2667FF | Middle row of the R mark, primary actions, and comparison graphics |
| Vivid coral | #FF6B5E | Lower row of the R mark, warm accents, and opposing comparison series |
| Off-white | #F5F7FB | Primary R shape, wordmark, and tagline on navy; light content canvas |

The approved concept image defines the primary color treatment: an off-white upper
R and right leg, electric-blue middle row, and coral lower row on midnight navy.
The wordmark and tagline are off-white on that navy background. Use this treatment
for the primary brand presentation and favicon.

For light surfaces, a secondary version changes the off-white artwork to navy and
retains the blue and coral rows. One-color versions are reserved for applications
that require them. The secondary version does not replace the primary treatment.
The supplied monochrome exports use pure black `#000000` or pure white `#FFFFFF`.

Use spacious, light content areas underneath a bold navy brand header. Color
should emphasize choices and comparisons while keeping dense matchup information
easy to read. Keep coral accents restrained. Use navy text on coral; verify final
text/background combinations during implementation. Labels must make sense
without color.

Typography: use **Barlow Condensed Bold (700)** for page and section headlines,
upright and in sentence case. Use the existing Inter/system sans-serif stack for
body copy, buttons, controls, and tables, with tabular numerals for probability
comparisons. The production wordmark uses condensed, slanted Anton outlines; the
tagline uses Inter outlines. The logo's outlined lettering is fixed artwork.

Logo: an abstract R built from three parallel forms suggesting stadium seating
rows and forward motion. Production artwork includes horizontal and stacked
arrangements, a compact mark, one-color versions, and derived favicon files.

Graphic language: precise matchup panels, clear percentage bars, strong alignment,
small league labels, and restrained motion inspired by broadcast graphics.

## Product boundaries

The current product provides probabilities, schedules/results, matchup comparisons,
and team-strength estimates. The clubhouse personality sets the tone only; the product
has no chat, hosted pools, leaderboards, or saved picks.

Only promote leagues that are actually supported. Label concept data as illustrative.
Do not suggest live scores or real-time predictions: the app uses a result only after
the game is final, and NFL results wait until the next day. Model explanations must
match the documented inputs; rosters, injuries, score margins, and betting markets
are not used.

## Decision log

- Audience: everyday sports fans and pool players who want straightforward help choosing winners.
- Scope: NFL and MLB, with other sports leagues planned.
- Personality: lively, social, and competitive; a game-day clubhouse.
- Betting is not the focus.
- Style: modern sports broadcast, with bold typography and crisp graphics.
- Naming: suggestive.
- Rally Row is the selected brand name, confirmed after reviewing availability findings.
- Preserve the combination of fan emotion and useful analysis from the Roar & Reason concept, but not that name.
- Drop the Bragging Room direction and bragging-led messaging.
- The first visual direction is approved: navy, electric blue, coral, bold broadcast lettering, and the abstract R concept.
- Primary color treatment: off-white upper R, wordmark, and tagline on navy, with blue and coral rows, matching the approved image.
- Selected tagline: "A little insight. A lot to talk about."
- Approved homepage headline: "What's your call?"
- Homepage supporting copy and the "Explore matchups" button label are approved.
- Approved short description: "Rally Row helps everyday sports fans see how teams stack up. It gives them the numbers to make informed calls."
- Methodology summary is approved: past-season ratings, updates as the current season progresses, and matchup comparisons showing each team's chance of winning.
- Interface headline typography: Barlow Condensed Bold (700), upright and in sentence case; Inter for body text, buttons, and data.
